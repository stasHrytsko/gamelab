This started as a simple combination: take the satisfying organisation of Water Sort and connect it to a board where pieces slide until they hit something.

The picture was immediate. Colours move through exits into containers. The problem was finding a reason for those two spaces to depend on each other.

## The first version

Each exit led to one container. The player moved coloured blocks across the board and sent matching colours to the same place. It was clean, but once the destination of each colour was chosen, the puzzle became predictable.

The sorting lived at the edge of the board. It did not really change the movement puzzle inside it.

## Making mistakes reversible

The useful change was allowing blocks to come back from a container. A container was no longer a finish line. It became temporary storage, and its top block became another available move.

That created a small tension: returning a block can solve the order inside a container, but it also puts something back on the board and may block the exit you need next.

## What survived

The game is no longer a direct mix of two references. Most of the original rules disappeared. What survived was the question that made the combination interesting:

> Can the player think about board space and container order as one connected problem?

That question eventually became The Gap.
