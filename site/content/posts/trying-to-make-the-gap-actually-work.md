---

The Gap changed again. The first version had movement and sorting, but the two parts felt like neighbours rather than one game. I could solve the board, then think about the containers. That separation made every move feel flatter than the idea sounded on paper.

## What felt wrong

Moving blocks was readable, but too passive. Once I assigned a colour to an exit, much of the level became execution. The interesting decision had already happened, and the rest was just carrying it out.

The containers also started empty. That made them feel like score buckets rather than part of the puzzle. Nothing inside them could surprise me or force a change of plan.

> If two mechanics can be solved independently, mixing them does not automatically create a new mechanic.

## What I’m testing now

The containers start partially filled and mixed. A block can move from the board into a container, but it can also come back out. That return changes the board, occupies an exit and can expose a different block inside the container.

Now the same move affects both spaces:

- where blocks can slide on the board;
- which colour is accessible in a container;
- whether an exit stays free for the next move;
- how many moves remain before the level becomes inefficient.

The important part is not that the game has more rules. It is that one decision now has consequences in two places.

## The current question

I’m watching for planned returns. If players only take blocks back after an obvious mistake, the mechanic is still cleanup. If they return a block before they are forced to, because they can see what it unlocks later, then the idea may finally be doing what I wanted.

That is the version I’m building now. It may change again.
